# 4.7 Warranty Claims

> Recovers the cost of warranty work — failed parts and the labour to replace them — from the supplier or manufacturer. Part of [Module 4: Workshop Management](../04-workshop-management.md).

## Purpose

When a water pump fitted four months ago starts weeping on a customer's Toyota Hilux 2.8 GD-6, the customer pays nothing — but somebody must, and it should be the supplier who sold the pump, not the workshop. A warranty claim packages the evidence (which part, from which GRN/batch, fitted on which job, failed at what mileage and date, with what symptoms), the costs being claimed (replacement part plus labour hours at the agreed claim rate), and tracks the supplier's response from `submitted` through `acknowledged`, `approved`/`rejected`, to `credited` when the supplier's credit note (Module 3.5) lands.

Without this discipline, warranty work is silent margin destruction: the job card shows zero revenue against real cost, and nobody chases the recovery. With it, warranty cost sits visibly on the job (4.6) offset by the expected recovery, aged claims appear on a chase list, and per-supplier claim statistics (claim rate, approval rate, average settlement days) feed straight into buying decisions — a brand whose pumps fail at 3% and whose distributor rejects half the claims is expensive at any price.

The physical side runs in parallel: most suppliers demand the failed part back for inspection, which is a warranty-reason return to supplier (Module 3.6) cross-linked to the claim.

## Workflow

```
Failure presented (customer returns / found in service)
        │
        ▼
Verify: part supplied & fitted by us? within warranty period/mileage?
        │
   ┌────┴────────────┐
   ▼                 ▼
eligible        not eligible ──► normal chargeable job (advisor informs customer)
   │
   ▼
Warranty job card (4.1, job_type = warranty)
  replacement part + labour lines flagged is_warranty
        │
        ▼
Create claim: failed part · supplier · failure description ·
              failure date & mileage · costs claimed (part + labour hrs)
        │
        ▼
Submit to supplier ──► supplier_claim_ref captured
        │                          │
        ▼                          ▼
Failed part returned (3.6,     status: acknowledged ──► approved / rejected
warranty reason, cross-linked)              │                   │
        │                                   ▼                   ▼
        │                          credit note (3.5)      manager decision:
        │                                   │             resubmit / dispute /
        ▼                                   ▼             write off
   inspection outcome              status: credited
```

1. **Verify** — Eligibility check: the part must trace to our sale/fitment (serial/batch from 4.4 issue records, or invoice lookup) and fall within the supplier's warranty terms (months and/or mileage).
2. **Do the work** — A `warranty` job card carries the replacement; its parts and labour lines are `is_warranty`, excluded from customer billing.
3. **Claim** — The foreman raises the claim with failure narrative, failure date/mileage (odometer from 4.5 history), photos, and claimed values: part at cost, labour at the supplier's claim rate.
4. **Submit & chase** — The clerk submits (portal/email per supplier), records `supplier_claim_ref`, and works the aging list; the failed part ships back under an RMA (3.6).
5. **Settle** — Approval leads to a credit note captured in 3.5, which flips the claim to `credited` and posts the recovery against the job's cost (4.6). Rejection routes to the manager: resubmit with better evidence, dispute, or write off.

## Fields & Validation

| Field | Type | Required | Validation rules |
|-------|------|----------|------------------|
| job_id | lookup | Yes | Job card with `is_warranty` lines; one claim per failed part per job |
| part_id | lookup | Yes | The failed part; must have sale/issue traceability to this business |
| supplier_id | select | Yes | Supplier of the failed part (from source GRN); override needs manager approval |
| failure_date | date | Yes | ≥ original fitment date; ≤ today |
| failure_mileage | int | Yes | ≥ odometer at fitment (from original job); plausibility warning vs vehicle history |
| failure_description | textarea | Yes | Min 30 chars; photos strongly prompted |
| labour_hrs_claimed | decimal | Yes | ≤ book time for the replacement operation (4.2); claim rate from supplier terms |
| parts_cost_claimed | decimal | Auto | Replacement part at cost; editable down only |
| supplier_claim_ref | text | Before `acknowledged` | Max 100 |
| credit_amount | decimal | At settlement | ≥ 0; shortfall vs total_claimed computed |

## Business Rules

1. Eligibility is evidence-based: the claim must link the failed part to a source GRN (proving which supplier) and to the original fitment job or sale (proving warranty start date/mileage). No traceability, no claim — the manager may still authorise goodwill, which is a different cost category.
2. Warranty terms are held per supplier (and optionally per part category): months, mileage, and the labour claim rate the supplier honours; the claim form enforces them and warns on out-of-terms submissions.
3. Claimed labour is capped at the operation's book time (4.2) — suppliers pay book, never blown-out actuals.
4. A claim's `credited` status requires a linked supplier credit note (3.5); partial credits leave the claim `approved` with the shortfall on the short-settled list until written off or topped up.
5. The failed part must be quarantined, not binned: the linked 3.6 return tracks its journey; suppliers reject claims when the evidence was scrapped.
6. Customer-facing invoice (4.8) excludes all warranty lines; if the failure is out of warranty, nothing here applies — it is a normal chargeable job.
7. Write-offs require the manager's reason and post the unrecovered cost to a warranty-expense account, keeping job-margin reporting honest (4.6).
8. Claim statistics per supplier (claims raised, approval %, average settlement days, value recovered) are computed rolling 12 months and surface in supplier reviews and price comparison (3.8).

## Edge Cases & Error Handling

- **Part bought from us but fitted elsewhere** (retail counter sale, customer's own mechanic fitted): parts-only claim — labour is not claimable and the form disables labour fields when no original fitment job exists; the customer's recourse for fitting labour is the fitting mechanic.
- **Consequential damage** (failed pump cooked the head gasket): the base claim covers the pump; consequential-damage claims are a separate negotiated claim flagged `consequential`, requiring manager involvement and supplier pre-approval before repair work proceeds.
- **Supplier no longer traded with / insolvent**: claim can be created for record-keeping but flags "no active supplier account"; manager decides write-off vs pursuing the manufacturer directly.
- **Failure mileage below fitment mileage** (odometer replaced or typo): hard validation stops it; the override path requires the odometer-override note from vehicle history (4.5).
- **Supplier approves labour but rejects the part** (claims misfitting): partial approval recorded per component; the rejected portion routes to manager decision with the inspection report attached.
- **Same batch failing repeatedly** (three alternators from GRN-20260410-0033): batch-level flag raised after N claims on one batch; remaining stock of that batch is quarantined pending supplier recall/inspection — a claims pattern becoming a stock action.
- **Customer demands the failed part back**: warranty evidence must go to the supplier; the advisor's script and the job-card flag (4.1 "return old parts") explicitly exclude warranty parts, and the claim record is the audit trail for why.
- **Claim aged > 60 days without acknowledgement**: escalation appears on the chase list with the supplier's warranty-contact details and one-click chase logging.
